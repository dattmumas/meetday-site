How a set is judged Pro

Three lamps, one verdict

A meet has three judges. Meet Day has three lamps for each set you declare. White is a pass. Red is a fail.

  • REPSWhite when you did every declared rep.
  • LOADWhite when the bar was at the declared load. For an assisted lift, at most the declared load.
  • EFFORTWhite at RPE 8 or less. No RPE counts as a pass.

A set with no red lamp is a good lift. One red lamp makes it a no lift. The reason shows under the verdict. Warm-ups are not judged.

The lights are part of Meet Day Pro. Without Pro, you log the same sets and see no verdict.

Questions

Short answers

Which iPhones?

iPhones on iOS 17 and later. The AI trainer needs iOS 27.

What is a good lift?

With Meet Day Pro, a declared set with three white lamps is a good lift. One red lamp is a no lift. Warm-ups and sets with no declared load are not judged.

Do I have to rate my effort?

No. A set without an RPE passes the effort lamp. RPE 9 or 10 turns it red.

Is the import free?

Yes. The import, logging, workouts and history are free. Meet Day Pro adds the lights and the card.

Does the trainer give medical advice?

No. The AI trainer (Pro, iOS 27) keeps to your training and your log. For pain or an injury, it tells you to stop that movement and see a doctor or a physical therapist.

Where is my data?

In a database at Supabase, on Amazon Web Services. It is encrypted in transit and at rest, and no other account can read it. The app has no ads and no analytics SDKs. The privacy policy has the details.

Can I delete my account?

Yes. Today > Settings > Delete Account removes your account and all training data at once. It does not cancel a Meet Day Pro subscription.
